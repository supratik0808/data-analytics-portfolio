Data-driven workforce insights for strategic decision-making, built on a Power BI dashboard.

**Date:** June 2026
**Dataset:** 22,214 employee records
**Tools:** Power BI, Excel/SQL (data prep)
**Deliverables:** `PRDA-03_HR_Analysis_Report.docx`, `PRDA-03_Insights_PPT.pptx`

---

## Overview

This project analyzes a 22,214-record HR dataset to surface workforce trends across hiring, gender, department, race, and geography, and to turn those trends into prioritized recommendations for the company.

## KPI Summary

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total Employees | 22,214 |
| Average Age | 38.43 years |
| Remote Workers | 24.75% |
| HQ Workers | 75.25% |
| Attrition Rate | 17.69% |

**Key observation:** A predominantly mid-career, office-based workforce with limited remote flexibility — an opportunity area for talent attraction.

## Key Insights

1. **Hiring Trend** — Grew from 220 hires in 2000 to a stable 1,000–1,140/year from 2002 onward; dipped to 1,012 in 2020 (COVID-19 impact).
2. **Gender Distribution** — Male 50.81%, Female 46.46%, Non-Conforming 2.72% — broadly balanced.
3. **Department** — Engineering leads (~6,700, ~30%), followed by Accounting (~3,300); Sales, HR, and Training are balanced at 1,700–1,800 each.
4. **Race** — White employees highest (~6,300, 28%); Black/African American, Asian, and Two-or-More-Races near-equal (~3,600 each); Hispanic/Latino lowest (~2,500).
5. **State** — Ohio dominates with ~18,000 employees (81% of workforce); Pennsylvania a distant second (~1,100) — a geographic concentration risk.

## Strategic Recommendations

**High Priority**
- Expand remote work options (currently only 24.75%)
- Launch a post-COVID hiring recovery strategy (campus recruitment, referrals)
- Increase diversity hiring, especially Hispanic/Latino and Non-Conforming representation

**Medium Priority**
- Geographic expansion beyond Ohio (satellite offices in IL, PA, MI)
- Build a younger-talent pipeline via internships and campus drives

**Long-Term**
- Female leadership development and mentorship programs
- Regular exit interviews to address the 17.69% attrition rate
- Continuous upskilling for Engineering, the largest department

## Conclusion

The workforce is stable and experienced, with reasonably balanced gender and racial diversity, but faces three clear risks: heavy geographic concentration in Ohio, low remote-work adoption, and a hiring slowdown since 2020. Addressing these through geographic, diversity, and flexibility-focused initiatives is the core of the strategic plan above.
